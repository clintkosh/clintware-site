The work I am built for

Multiple proof points for the things this role needs.

My background is hard to fit into a narrow title because the value comes from the overlap: customer operations, technical problem solving, enablement, product thinking, change management, workflow automation, and now hands-on LLM and agent work.

Embed + audit

Learn how the team actually works before automating it.

I have spent years doing this with customers and internal teams where the documented process and the real process are not the same thing.

  • Mapped fragmented Check Point / Avanan customer state into a usable operating view and improved portfolio visibility from roughly 40% to 99%.
  • Customer Success work across onboarding, adoption, Support, Product, Engineering, Sales, executive stakeholders, and renewal.
  • Pattern recognition around weak signals that often show up in conversations and behavior before they appear in KPIs.
Build v1 fast

Move from problem to working first version without waiting for perfect conditions.

AI now lets me research, prototype, test, and revise almost continuously. I use that speed to learn faster, not to skip judgment.

  • RenewNudge turns a recurring Customer Success visibility problem into a working application.
  • 19 published applications/tools across customer systems, AI workflows, local utilities, security, and operating infrastructure.
  • Local stack includes Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini, Grok, Ollama, Open WebUI, n8n, Docker, SearXNG, and PowerShell automation.
Quality gates

Know when AI is helping, and know when it is producing slop.

I care about AI slop in the broad sense: model drift, compounded coding errors, bad assumptions, and plausible output that no longer matches the source of truth.

  • Use rollback, source-of-truth checks, smaller scopes, deterministic validation, model switching, rubrics, and human approval when a run gets away from the goal.
  • RenewNudge is the stable design reference if you want to judge whether I can bring rapid AI-assisted development back under deliberate human control.
  • Clintware is intentionally iterative, so unfinished experiments remain visibly unfinished rather than being presented as polished production work.
Reusable assets

Every solved problem should make the next one easier.

I naturally turn repeated knowledge into a skill, template, workflow, evaluation pattern, or approved capability.

  • 18 implemented skills: 17 published and 1 internal.
  • 1 public plugin package with its own manifest and reusable skill.
  • MCP.Clintware is being built so capabilities, identity, permissions, and context can outlive whichever model uses them.
Teach to self-sufficiency

I like teaching, and I do not want the team dependent on me.

I am comfortable starting with a simple analogy or going all the way down into the technical details, depending on the person in front of me.

  • At the Bush School, used analogies such as a library card catalog to make concepts like the Windows Registry understandable, then went deeper as needed.
  • At Cofense, mentored Support Engineers and built knowledge-sharing and self-service approaches around Reporter.
  • Built a Reporter Installer Customizer so teammates could perform a technically detailed packaging task without first learning everything I knew.
Measure impact

The metric is what improved for the people doing the work.

I am not interested in counting prompts or agents if they do not change the outcome.

  • Dedrone: +20% renewals, +25% engagement, onboarding standardized to roughly one month.
  • Check Point / Avanan: roughly 30% team-efficiency improvement and portfolio visibility from about 40% to 99%.
  • Cofense: roughly 25% faster critical-issue resolution and substantial ticket reduction/deflection through better process and knowledge.
Systems thinking

See the connected system, then decide which parts need AI.

I do not need an agent to win the architecture argument. The team needs to win.

  • Use deterministic logic when deterministic logic is better.
  • Use n8n as connective tissue for triggers, APIs, transformations, approvals, and actions.
  • Use an LLM or agent only where interpretation, synthesis, ambiguity, or recurring variable reasoning justifies it.
Change leadership

Build with people, not at them.

My Customer Success career is fundamentally an adoption career: understand the goal, remove friction, create confidence, make the new way easier, and keep listening after launch.

  • QBRs/EBRs, onboarding, office hours, adoption reviews, risk interventions, executive alignment, and escalation leadership.
  • Servant-leadership approach focused on removing barriers and growing the judgment of the team around me.
  • Comfortable operating in ambiguity and high-pressure war-room environments where I become more focused as the environment becomes noisier.

This is my work and how I think

I start with the outcome, find the pattern, build the smallest useful thing, and leave a path behind me.

Outcome before technology.

The first question is not “Where can we use AI?” It is “What are we trying to make better?” AI only earns a place when it improves the work.

Signal before dashboard.

Some of the most important customer and operational signals appear in language, behavior, workarounds, stakeholder changes, and repeated friction before they become formal metrics.

Speed with judgment.

I like being able to build almost as fast as I think, but I also know when to stop a bad run, inspect the source of truth, roll back, change context, or switch models.

Teach the path.

A useful workflow should leave the next person more capable. A prompt becomes a skill, a workflow becomes a template, a quality decision becomes an eval, and repeated knowledge stops living in one person's head.

Journey + milestones

The same pattern keeps showing up in different forms.

The technologies change. The operating instinct is consistent: understand the real problem, create structure, make the work visible, teach what I learn, and improve the system around the people doing the job.
