## Community job

The MindVergent™ community is not a promotional Discord for Clintware. It exists to generate and test the first contribution history behind the professional-trust thesis.

The site is the durable product / knowledge layer. Discord is the fast conversation layer.

Site = research, canonical summaries, artifacts, future contribution profiles, attribution, search, and later trust-driven collaboration.
Discord = introductions, questions, live discussion, peer review, build sessions, and community relationships.

**MindVergent™ Labs** is the research and experimentation surface that seeds useful work into both layers.

## Welcome message

Welcome to MindVergent™.

This is an early community testing a simple idea: professional trust should come from useful work and what other people can actually verify about it, not only what a profile says.

Start in #introductions with four things:
1. What do you work on or build?
2. What can people ask you about?
3. What are you trying to learn or find right now?
4. Share one useful thing you have learned recently.

This is intentionally small. Useful contribution matters more than audience size.

## Rules

1. Be useful before being promotional.
2. Challenge ideas, not people.
3. Give attribution when someone improves your work.
4. Disclose when you have a financial/commercial interest in something you recommend.
5. No unsolicited mass DMs, scraping members, or lead harvesting.
6. Do not repost private community material externally without permission.
7. Evidence and lived experience are welcome; distinguish them from speculation.
8. Different working styles and ways of thinking are part of the room by design.
9. Jobs, services, products, and partnerships are allowed in their channels after you have introduced yourself and contributed.
10. Moderators can remove low-effort promotion, harassment, manipulation, or repeated bad-faith behavior.

## Founding-member standard

The first 10-20 members should be manually invited.

Optimize for:

- people who build or improve something;
- people with specific operating experience;
- people willing to answer and ask real questions;
- different specialties with enough overlap to help each other;
- low ego / high evidence;
- members who will test the community rather than simply lurk forever.

Do not optimize for follower count.

## First invitations

Invite a mixed group rather than 20 people from the same background:

- 3-4 AI / automation builders;
- 3-4 Customer Success / Support / operations practitioners;
- 2-3 founders;
- 2-3 developers / technical builders;
- 2 product or design thinkers;
- 2 people who are unusually good at research, documentation, or synthesizing complex information.

Overlap is fine.

## First four recurring rituals

### Monday — What are you solving?
One thread. Members post one real problem they are working through this week.

### Wednesday — Field note
One member shares a short lesson from something they actually built, operated, sold, supported, tested, or broke.

### Friday — Peer review
One workflow, product idea, prompt, process, article, or artifact gets constructive review.

### Monthly — Build / collaboration thread
Members post something they want to build, test, find, or help with. This becomes the earliest signal for whether trust can create collaboration.

## Seed discussion prompts

1. Where should probabilistic AI reasoning stop and deterministic controls begin?
2. What workflow are you still doing manually because current software misunderstands the real job?
3. Which Customer Success health signal has actually changed an action you took?
4. What did you build recently that taught you something the documentation did not?
5. What makes you trust someone's technical or professional advice online?
6. When does a useful prompt deserve to become a reusable workflow or product?
7. What tool do people recommend constantly that does not work well for your actual process?
8. What is one thing you are unusually good at that does not fit neatly into your job title?

## Content-to-community loop

For each MindVergent™ Labs research post:

1. Publish founder research on the public site.
2. Open a matching Discord discussion.
3. Capture useful corrections, counterexamples, and implementation notes.
4. Update the canonical site article with attribution.
5. Record contributors and the type of contribution.
6. Later use this history as the first raw material for contribution profiles and domain-specific reputation.

This prevents useful knowledge from dying inside chat while keeping the conversation fast.

## Promotion rule

Clintware products may be discussed when directly relevant, but they follow the same standard as member products.

Good:
- a product is used as a real example in a discussion;
- asking for testers in #looking-for-testers;
- sharing a release in #show-your-work;
- offering a disclosed partner benefit.

Bad:
- turning #general into a Clintware feed;
- automatically pinning Clintware products above member work;
- hiding affiliate or commercial relationships;
- using membership as a lead list.

The community must remain valuable to someone who never buys anything from Clintware.

## What to measure during the first month

Track manually in a simple table:

- invited;
- joined;
- introduced themselves;
- first substantive contribution;
- second contribution within 14 days;
- replied to another member;
- artifact or answer improved by someone else;
- collaboration/test/referral created;
- member named another member they would trust for a specific task and why.

Do not optimize for total member count yet.

## When to build site-native forums

Do not build forums just because forums are in the roadmap.

Build the site-native discussion layer when at least one is true:

- valuable Discord threads are repeatedly difficult to recover;
- Labs research consistently produces useful discussion worth indexing;
- attribution/revision history becomes cumbersome manually;
- members ask for public searchable answers;
- contribution history is beginning to affect trust decisions.

Until then Discord is a cheaper test.

## When to introduce commerce

Only after members already choose collaborators based on trust created inside MindVergent™.

First transaction experiments should be manual and narrow:

- paid expert call;
- small project referral;
- paid template/workflow;
- design-partner introduction;
- sponsored tool test with disclosure.

If members do not naturally want to work with each other, adding a marketplace UI will not fix the underlying problem.
