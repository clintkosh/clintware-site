## Overall fit

Quillgeist remains the primary pitch because it is the most technically complete and founder-authentic venture.  BuyerOrigin now earns the number two position because it has a narrow economic buyer, measurable dollar value, a working audit, and a fast path to paid design partners.  LandThePlane moves to number three because it is useful and working but currently lacks the same direct paid-business wedge.

The website should reflect one ordered portfolio, not three simultaneous flagships:

1. Quillgeist: primary YC company.
2. BuyerOrigin: commercial challenger with a working MVP.
3. LandThePlane: active career-system product track.

## Funding fit and use

YC currently publishes a $500,000 standard deal: $125,000 for 7% and $375,000 on an uncapped MFN SAFE.  The public deal page does not publish a product-specific restriction on spending.  Company funds should nevertheless be used for documented company purposes, governed through the company, kept separate from personal spending, and reviewed against signed financing documents and professional legal/accounting advice.

For a single accepted company, reasonable general corporate uses may include founder runway, product engineering, cloud and security costs, privacy/compliance work, merchant pilots, customer development, and hiring.  Do not present YC funding as a portfolio grant that can casually finance unrelated Clintware experiments.  If Quillgeist applies and is funded, BuyerOrigin work must have a defensible relationship to the same company or remain separately funded and separately accounted for.
