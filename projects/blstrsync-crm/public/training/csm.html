Own the outcome. Share the technical truth.

Candidate-built training prototype. The CSM lens keeps customer objectives, stakeholders, adoption, risk, commercial context, and technical dependencies aligned without asking the customer to coordinate internal teams.

Open system

CSM definition of done

The customer can state the outcome, knows who owns each open commitment, sees technical risk early, and receives one coherent plan from CSM + TAM. Expansion follows verified value, not a generic usage score.

Customer Success Trust
1

Start with the customer outcome

Before features, write the problem the customer is trying to reduce or the operating capability they are trying to build. Capture the stakeholder who owns that outcome and the evidence they will accept.

  • Outcome
  • Owner
  • Evidence criterion
  • Decision date / checkpoint
2

Keep one shared account state

The CSM owns the success plan and relationship context. The TAM owns technical depth. Both read the same journey, risk, commitments, protected-asset/integration state, and next decision.

CUSTOMER OUTCOME -> SHARED CONTEXT -> CSM BUSINESS OWNER + TAM TECHNICAL OWNER -> ONE CUSTOMER PLAN

3

Pull the TAM at the right moment

Bring the TAM in when configuration, API/integration behavior, monitoring/takedown tuning, technical enablement, or advanced troubleshooting materially affects the customer outcome. Do not wait until the issue becomes an executive escalation.

4

Run risk as a decision system

Every risk needs customer impact, current evidence, owner, next decision, and communication state. Avoid vague red/yellow/green labels with no action.

RISK -> IMPACT -> EVIDENCE -> OWNER -> NEXT DECISION -> NEXT CUSTOMER UPDATE

5

Expand from evidence

For an adjacent use case, first establish the customer problem and what evidence would make it worth operationalizing. Then involve the TAM for technical validation. Bring Sales/commercial context in after value is defendable.

PROBLEM -> EVIDENCE CRITERION -> TECHNICAL VALIDATION -> PROVEN VALUE -> COMMERCIAL MOTION

Never do this

  • Do not invent ROI or expansion dollars.
  • Do not present a synthetic demo metric as a customer result.
  • Do not let CSM and TAM run separate customer truths.
  • Do not wait for the customer to discover an ownership gap.

Values in the workflow

Trust: source-backed claims and visible commitments. Think Big: repeated friction becomes scalable improvement. Mutual Respect: preserve context during disagreement and handoff. Customer Success: customer-defined outcome is the anchor.
