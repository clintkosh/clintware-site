SYNERGY CONSOLE · PARTNER ENABLEMENT

Route once. Preserve context. Accept ownership.

Candidate-built training prototype for a tiered partner-support ecosystem. The purpose is not to flatten service boundaries; it is to make them usable without forcing the customer to navigate the org chart.

Open system

Partner definition of done

For every issue, the correct service lane is clear, entitlement/context travels with the transfer, the receiving owner explicitly accepts or identifies a missing prerequisite, and recurring boundary problems are fed back into the system.

Mutual Respect Trust
1

Identify the lane

ACCOUNT / BILLING / USER MANAGEMENT -> SERVICE ACCOUNT OWNER BASELINE BREAK-FIX -> PARTNER SUPPORT PURCHASED SETUP / CONFIG / TRAINING -> INTEGRATION PACKAGE DEEP / NAMED TECHNICAL SERVICE -> PRO / MANAGED SERVICES UNCOVERED PRODUCT-DEPTH GAP -> VENDOR TAM REPRODUCIBLE PRODUCT DEFECT -> PRODUCT / ENGINEERING VIA EVIDENCE-READY ESCALATION

2

Carry the handoff packet

Do not transfer only a ticket number. Carry the customer outcome/impact, entitlement or package, issue classification, what has been tried, evidence/logs/examples, current owner, exact next ask, and closure condition.

3

Require acceptance

The receiving team either accepts ownership or identifies the exact missing prerequisite. A transfer is not complete when the sending team removes itself from the queue.

SEND CONTEXT -> RECEIVE / REVIEW -> ACCEPT OWNER OR NAME MISSING INPUT -> CUSTOMER UPDATE -> NEXT CHECKPOINT

4

Keep the customer from repeating the story

Internal ownership can change. Customer context should not. Preserve the summary, impact, evidence, and next decision through every handoff.

Mutual Respect
5

Turn boundary pain into system improvement

Tag repeated transfers, entitlement confusion, configuration questions, and missing documentation. Review patterns with the TAM and customer-success owner so the durable fix lands in documentation, enablement, automation, service packaging, pricing, or Product.

Think Big

Never do this

  • Do not route by job title alone. Route by issue type, entitlement, evidence, and needed outcome.
  • Do not use “out of scope” as the final customer answer.
  • Do not duplicate discovery after every transfer.
  • Do not silently perform recurring out-of-scope work without feeding the pattern back into service design.

Values in the workflow

Trust: honest service boundaries and evidence. Think Big: repeated issues improve the system. Mutual Respect: clean, contextual handoffs. Customer Success: judge routing by the customer's path to an outcome, not internal queue ownership.
