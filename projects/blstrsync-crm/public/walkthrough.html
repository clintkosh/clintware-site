Walkthrough of a synthetic customer-motion system designed around the public dual-motion TAM problem: direct enterprise + partner-sourced customers, CSM/TAM coordination, and tiered partner support boundaries.

Open system
The problem

Customer context fractures when relationship ownership, technical ownership, partner entitlement, support cases, services, product escalation, and adoption live in separate queues. The system does not replace those source systems. It synchronizes the decision layer around the customer.

Mutual Respect

1. Command Center

Open one synthetic account and see motion, lifecycle, CSM, TAM, partner lane, workflow progress, customer objective, and the next decision. Progress is synthetic workflow completion, never product efficacy.

2. Team + track dropdowns

Change Team Lens to CSM, TAM, or Partner. The Track selector narrows to work relevant to that team while keeping the same underlying account state. This is role-specific focus without separate databases.

3. Customer Journey

Every case, integration, and risk sits inside a lifecycle sequence. The journey answers why the task exists and what decision it is trying to unlock.

4. Handoff Contract

A handoff contains from/to owner, service lane, problem/impact summary, evidence, acceptance state, and next decision. The receiver accepts ownership instead of the sender merely dropping the ticket.

CONTEXT -> EVIDENCE -> OWNER ASK -> ACCEPT / MISSING PREREQUISITE -> CUSTOMER UPDATE

5. Technical + Threat State

The TAM view connects integrations, troubleshooting evidence, protected assets, monitoring, enforcement/takedown state, and customer maintenance. It separates advanced product work from baseline support and paid services rather than flattening everything into “support.”

6. Adoption + recurring themes

Adoption metrics are source-labeled synthetic examples. Expansion is gated behind a customer problem and evidence criterion. Recurring issues aggregate toward documentation, enablement, automation, packaging/pricing, or Product.

Think Big

7. Training Center

CSM, TAM, and Partner guides teach the same ownership model the workspace enforces. Training is not separate from the operating system.

8. Evidence + local-first build model

The Evidence page separates customer data from internal build evidence. A completed internal Quillgeist benchmark measured 2.996s repository refresh, 22.220s validate/materialize/check/deploy, and 13.560s browser verification. The associated 30k-60k token reduction is a directional model, not billing telemetry or a financial-savings claim.

Evidence boundary

This entire customer dataset is synthetic. Public role/company information informs the operating model, but the system does not claim knowledge of private internal tooling, reporting lines, customer data, or partner procedures.
