# LandThePlane Crucible Preparation Standard

## Purpose

LandThePlane must prepare the candidate deeply enough that one ambiguous question, one interviewer-specific preference, or one imperfectly retrieved example does not erase the candidate's broader evidence.

The standard is not "prepare less" and it is not "memorize scripts." It is:

**PREPARE DEEPER → INDEX BETTER → ANSWER NARROWER → PROVE MORE**

Each company and interviewer is modeled independently. Signals from one interviewer may inform the evidence graph, but must not silently recalibrate the preparation strategy for a different interviewer, company, role, or competency.

## Required preparation pipeline

For every serious interview stage:

1. **Scorecard reconstruction**
   - Extract explicit competencies from recruiter guides, job descriptions, interview instructions, prior-round feedback, and interviewer context.
   - Separate documented criteria from inferred likely criteria.
   - Never invent an interviewer preference.

2. **Evidence mapping**
   - Map each competency to verified candidate evidence.
   - Maintain one primary story and at least one backup where evidence allows.
   - Preserve metrics, scope, stakeholders, ownership, and outcome.
   - Missing proof remains a gap.

3. **Answer ladder**
   - 30-second framework answer.
   - 60–90 second evidence-backed answer.
   - Executive version emphasizing outcome, tradeoff, scale, and role relevance.
   - Do not require word-for-word memorization.

4. **Ambiguity protection**
   - Identify questions with multiple plausible scopes: self vs team, philosophy vs operating framework, strategy vs execution, customer vs internal stakeholder, personal resilience vs people-management practice.
   - Candidate should scope before answering when ambiguity is material.
   - Preferred rescue: "If you mean X, here's how I handle it. If you mean Y, I can address that too."

5. **Failure-mode preparation**
   Red-team at least:
   - framework vs philosophy;
   - self vs team;
   - scale and complexity;
   - people-management depth;
   - metrics and outcomes;
   - ownership vs contribution;
   - technical depth;
   - executive communication;
   - over-indexing on AI/tools when the competency is human judgment or relationship strength;
   - over-explaining;
   - desperation/reassurance seeking;
   - carrying one interviewer's preferences into another company's interview.

6. **Adversarial practice**
   - Interrupt answers.
   - Ask follow-up questions.
   - Deliberately use ambiguous wording.
   - Challenge scale, metrics, ownership, and weak assumptions.
   - Require the candidate to recover without inventing evidence.

7. **Readiness gate**
   A round is not marked PREP READY merely because an answer exists.
   Readiness requires:
   - material scorecard competencies identified;
   - evidence coverage visible;
   - primary/backup stories where available;
   - ambiguity traps identified;
   - scale/metric gaps visible;
   - red-team pass completed;
   - live-cockpit retrieval compressed enough for use under pressure.

8. **Post-round learning**
   - Compare what was actually asked with what was prepared.
   - Detect scope misunderstandings, framework misses, evidence gaps, and interviewer-specific corrections.
   - Add durable lessons to the career graph.
   - Do not generalize one interviewer's preference into a global rule without repeated evidence.

## Interviewer isolation invariant

**One interviewer does not overwrite another interviewer.**

Preparation for Interviewer B may use factual evidence learned from Interviewer A, but A's communication preference, emphasis, or reaction does not automatically become B's preparation strategy.

Examples:
- An AI-heavy interviewer does not make the next interviewer AI-heavy.
- A relationship-first interviewer does not justify reducing structured management-framework preparation for a later leadership interview.
- A conversational round does not lower preparation depth for a structured competency round.

## Retrieval format

The final cockpit should be substantially smaller than the preparation corpus.

For each competency, surface:

`COMPETENCY → FRAMEWORK → PRIMARY PROOF → METRIC → BACKUP → AMBIGUITY TRAP → ROLE LINK`

The preparation corpus may be extensive. The live surface should be compact.

## Communication integration

The Crucible Standard works with Communication Guard. Interview prep should also flag answer patterns that may unintentionally reduce perceived seniority or clarity:

- narrating anxiety or perceived mistakes;
- unnecessary personal-need disclosures;
- excessive praise/deference;
- repeated interest statements;
- overlong setup before answering;
- failing to answer the exact question;
- using personal tolerance as a substitute for a people-management framework.

## Product boundary

This is candidate-side coaching. It is not a validated hiring assessment and must not predict hiring outcomes. It should help the candidate surface truthful evidence and reduce avoidable communication loss.
