# ASTRO Website / Product Skill

This is the canonical Clintware website and product-positioning skill.  Read it before changing any public Clintware page, product page, startup / YC page, portfolio page, homepage, navigation, product positioning, or public-facing visual/copy system.

## Why this exists

The recurring failure mode is generic AI-generated landing-page output: oversized vague hero copy, repetitive card grids, empty SaaS claims, random metrics, excessive CTAs, fake polish, and roadmap language that reads like shipped capability.  Clintware must not look or read like a template.

ASTRO is the guardrail against that failure.

## Required sequence

Before changing a public site page:

1. Read this file.
2. Read `AGENTS.md` and `BRAND_STANDARDS.md`.
3. Inspect the current repository state and the actual page being changed.
4. Preserve approved working functionality, routes, downloads, forms, embeds, product links, analytics, security controls, and evidence.
5. Determine the page's real job before editing it.
6. Remove generic template structure where it obscures the real job.
7. Write from evidence and product truth, not marketing convention.
8. Distinguish clearly between what works now, what is being tested, and what is roadmap.
9. Validate links, page hierarchy, mobile readability, and deployment before calling the change complete.

Canonical sequence:

`READ ASTRO -> INSPECT LIVE/REPO STATE -> IDENTIFY PAGE JOB -> PRESERVE WORKING PARTS -> REMOVE TEMPLATE NOISE -> WRITE FROM EVIDENCE -> SEPARATE NOW/NEXT -> QA -> DEPLOY -> VERIFY`

## Clintware visual / writing constraints

- Use **Clintware™** and **GO FURTHEST.™** exactly where brand display is appropriate.
- No oversized bubbly, loopy, comic, generic SaaS, or generic AI-marketing hero typography.
- No giant headline simply because a marketing template expects one.
- Prefer compact, high-contrast, direct, senior/professional layouts.
- Pages should feel like a product brief, operator memo, technical dossier, professional profile, evidence record, or useful working surface depending on their purpose.
- Do not force every page into hero -> three cards -> feature grid -> CTA -> footer.
- Do not scatter random performance metrics without context.  Attach evidence to the company, role, product, decision, or operating change it proves.
- Avoid stacked vague descriptors, buzzwords, and unprovable superlatives.
- Use plain language that says what the thing does, why it exists, and what is true today.
- One clear primary action is better than repeated CTA buttons.
- Navigation should route by visitor intent, not expose every internal experiment equally.
- Do not mix CodeFEDDY / FEDDY branding into professional Clintware material.

## Page test

A first-time visitor should be able to answer quickly:

1. What is this page / product / profile?
2. Who is it for?
3. What problem or job does it address?
4. What exists or is proven today?
5. What is not yet proven or shipped?
6. Why should I care?
7. What is the one sensible next action?

If those answers are buried under marketing copy, the page fails.

## Product truth hierarchy

Use this order when presenting products:

`PROBLEM -> THESIS -> CURRENT PRODUCT -> EVIDENCE -> BOUNDARIES -> WHAT MUST BE PROVEN NEXT`

Not:

`TAGLINE -> BENEFIT CARDS -> GENERIC FEATURES -> REPEATED CTA`

Never present roadmap capability as current capability.  Label alpha, prototype, exploration, in-development, or future work accurately.

## Quillgeist-specific rule

Quillgeist is not merely a prompt optimizer, AI writing assistant, generic copilot, or browser wrapper.

Core positioning:

**Adaptive intent compiler for AI.**

Preserve the conceptual progression:

`TYPE -> UNDERSTAND -> IMPROVE -> EXECUTE -> LEARN`

Meaning:

- **TYPE:** the user expresses normal intent rather than constructing a perfect machine prompt.
- **UNDERSTAND:** Quillgeist resolves goal, context, constraints, tools, permissions, and definition of done.
- **IMPROVE:** it prepares the instruction/context needed by the target model or execution layer.
- **EXECUTE:** eligible deterministic work can run locally while model reasoning is used where judgment is actually needed.
- **LEARN:** accepted edits, rejected edits, corrections, preferences, and outcomes should eventually reduce repeated instruction.  This adaptive layer must remain clearly labeled as in development until demonstrated.

The product page should explain both the current local-execution proof and the larger adaptive-intent thesis.  Do not let the implementation details erase the product thesis, and do not let the thesis overstate the implementation.

## LandThePlane-specific rule

Do not position LandThePlane as another generic AI interview-question generator.

The durable asset is the **candidate-owned evidence graph / career evidence system**:

- verified accomplishments;
- target-role requirements;
- answer structures grounded in evidence;
- interview history and feedback;
- longitudinal improvement;
- post-hire ramp and accomplishment capture;
- credible future pivots based on demonstrated capability.

The initial wedge can be interview preparation and review because it provides urgency and feedback.  The product case depends on evidence reuse and repeat behavior across rounds and roles, not generic one-time resume analysis.

## Fall 2026 YC language

Quillgeist and LandThePlane may be described as **Fall 2026 Y Combinator application candidates** or **being prepared for a Fall 2026 YC application** when that is the current plan.

Do not imply acceptance, endorsement, affiliation, interview selection, or YC participation unless it actually occurs.

YC-oriented pages should read like founder/product evidence, not investor cosplay.  State:

- the problem;
- the wedge;
- what is built;
- what users can test now;
- what must be learned;
- what would falsify or strengthen the company thesis.

## Professional-profile rule

The professional profile must communicate Clinton Kosh's unusual operating range rather than a random list of metrics:

- direct customer and executive ownership;
- day-to-day Customer Success execution;
- technical escalation / Support bridge;
- Customer Success Operations and operating-system design;
- preventive work that often remains invisible because issues are caught before they become fires;
- cross-functional judgment across Customer Success, Support, Product, Engineering, Sales, Renewals, and implementation;
- ability to build the workflow, dashboard, automation, prototype, or tool that is missing.

Use manager/review evidence carefully.  Distinguish direct quotes, paraphrases, and second-hand reference feedback.  Scrub private/internal information where required.

## Final ASTRO rejection check

Before publishing, reject the draft if any of these are true:

- it could be swapped onto an unrelated AI startup with only the product name changed;
- the hero says almost nothing specific;
- the page has more marketing adjectives than evidence;
- every section is a card grid because that was easy to build;
- the page repeats the same CTA three or more times without a usability reason;
- roadmap items look shipped;
- important caveats are hidden in tiny text;
- the visitor has to infer what the product actually does;
- the page feels like generic SaaS boilerplate instead of Clintware.

If the draft fails, rewrite it before deployment.
