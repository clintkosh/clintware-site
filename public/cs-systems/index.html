Weak signals matter before the account turns red

Support
Recurring severity, reopenings, unresolved themes, and issues never raised in an EBR.Support history often exposes friction before the named champion frames it as a business risk.
Communication
Reply cadence, missed commitments, stakeholder absence, and changes in tone or engagement.Behavioral change is useful context when it is treated as a signal to investigate, not an automatic conclusion.
Usage
Adoption only matters in relation to the customer's intended outcome.A drop in usage means different things during implementation, after a workflow change, or when a champion leaves.
Cross-functional
Sales, Support, Product, Engineering, Renewals, and implementation all hold pieces of the account story.The operating system should bring those pieces together before a blind spot becomes a surprise.

Check Point / Avanan: scaled Customer Success in practice

Named ownership
20–40 accounts, about $3M in managed value, with recurring executive/business cadence.Success plans, usage and health reviews, QBRs/EBRs, technical risk, escalations, renewal readiness, and stakeholder alignment.
Shared coverage
Front-line customer and Sales triage across the broader HEC customer base.Email security, SSO, DLP, portal issues, onboarding, licensing questions, technical routing, and escalations, including customers without dedicated CSM coverage.
Visibility
Portfolio visibility improved from roughly 40% to about 99%.Customer state, ownership, risk, interaction history, and required next actions became easier to inspect.
Coverage
99% dedicated CSM coverage for enterprise customers over $50K and new-account outreach within a two-week SLA.The model separated strategic named ownership from scaled shared coverage rather than pretending every customer needed the same motion.

The invisible part of Customer Success

A calm account can be the output of substantial work upstream: a ticket pattern caught early, a technical issue routed before escalation, an internal disagreement resolved before the customer gets conflicting guidance, or a recurring failure turned into a playbook before the next customer reaches it.

Prevent
Catch the weak signal, clarify ownership, remove the blocker.Good preventive work often looks like nothing happened because the customer never experienced the larger failure.
Respond
When the fire exists, create one narrative and one decision path.Establish impact, assign owners, set communication cadence, keep the customer informed, and separate immediate recovery from root-cause prevention.
Learn
Convert the incident into a system improvement.Document where the first signal appeared and change the playbook, handoff, data, or automation so the same problem is caught earlier next time.

Operating rule

A health score without a next action is decoration. Every meaningful signal should lead to a decision, owner, or explicit choice to monitor.
