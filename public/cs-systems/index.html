CUSTOMER SUCCESS SYSTEMS

Customer Success should make the next action obvious before the fire becomes visible.

I build lightweight operating systems that keep customer objectives, adoption, risk, commitments, renewal timing, and ownership visible. The goal is not more process. The goal is fewer surprises, fewer avoidable escalations, and more problems solved before the customer has to feel them.

Professional profileOpen RenewNudge
The invisible layer

Preventive work can look like nothing happened.

That is often the point. A customer may never see the internal alignment, ticket review, stakeholder follow-up, technical triage, risk discussion, documentation work, or cross-functional coordination that prevented a concern from becoming a formal escalation. A calm account can be the output of substantial work upstream.

Operating principle

Do not wait for the renewal date or red account status to tell you there is a problem.

Changes in support demand, unresolved commitments, product friction, usage, response cadence, stakeholder participation, call tone, or who is suddenly missing from the conversation can reveal risk earlier than a dashboard alone.

Core motion

Six operating questions I want answered for every account.

01 · Outcome

What is the customer trying to accomplish?

Capture the original mission at the sale and keep the definition of done visible from implementation forward.

02 · Adoption

Are they using what they bought?

Measure meaningful usage and workflow adoption, not activity for its own sake.

03 · Risk

What can derail the relationship?

Technical, commercial, stakeholder, support, expectation, and behavioral risk should produce named actions before the issue compounds.

04 · Stakeholders

Who matters to the outcome?

Map champions, decision makers, technical owners, detractors, executive sponsors, and changes in participation over time.

05 · Renewal

What evidence supports the renewal?

Renewal readiness should accumulate throughout the relationship, not begin at the deadline.

06 · Next action

Who owns what happens next?

Every meaningful risk or commitment should have an owner, date, and visible follow-up.

In practice · Check Point / Avanan

This operating model came from having to serve both a strategic book and a much wider customer population.

At Check Point / Avanan, I was not only managing named Customer Success relationships. I also worked inside the shared operating layer that had to keep onboarding, customer questions, technical routing, escalations, renewals, data, and ownership moving across a rapidly growing HEC customer base.

Named book
Executive cadence + day-to-day ownershipMy directly managed portfolio was commonly described as roughly 20–40 customers and about $3M in account value, spanning smaller accounts through multi-million-dollar strategic relationships. I used success plans, usage and health reviews, QBRs / EBRs, stakeholder alignment, technical risk, escalation management, and renewal readiness to keep the customer mission connected to action.
Shared coverage
Customers outside a clean named-book model still needed an ownerI was a primary operating point for the shared HEC customer inquiry and onboarding motion. That included customer and Sales questions, licensing clarification, portal and product-policy issues, onboarding, renewal support, technical routing, and escalations, including many smaller customers or situations where there was not yet a dedicated CSM path.
Scale
Automation was necessary because the volume was realMy year-end tracking estimated 60–80% of a normal week in the inbox while I continued process and reporting work. Onboarding-related inbound work typically ran around 10–15 requests per day and reached the 20s–30s on heavy days. Customer records were entering the operating system in the hundreds per week, making structured triage, automation, and data visibility essential.
Playbooks
Turn repeated work into standard workThe operating environment was consolidated to roughly 20 production playbooks connected to templates, lifecycles, and automation. Twice-weekly office hours and reusable communications created a one-to-many adoption path while preserving direct help for exceptions.
Visibility
Move from fragmented context to a decision-ready picturePortfolio visibility improved from roughly 40% to about 99%, making customer state, risk, ownership, interaction history, and required next actions easier for Customer Success and the wider business to inspect.
Manager signal
Process improvement did not come at the expense of customer responsivenessA performance review described the Customer Success process work as impressive, explicitly recognized the extremely large volume of customer and field requests being balanced with it, and rated overall contribution above expectations. A later review also credited operational efficiency, customer satisfaction, and timely handling of the shared customer inquiry motion.
Signal layer

The system should connect what the customer says with what behavior and support history are already showing.

Structured data is the foundation. The next level is using the surrounding evidence to notice when the account story is changing.

Support
Look beyond ticket countsSeverity, recurrence, reopenings, time-to-resolution, unresolved themes, and issues voiced to Support but never raised in an EBR can expose friction that the named champion may not be surfacing.
Communication
Notice behavioral changeReply cadence, shorter responses, missed commitments, changes in tone, or a previously engaged stakeholder becoming absent can matter before anyone labels the account at risk.
Usage
Connect telemetry to the customer missionA drop in use only matters in context. Tie adoption changes back to the workflow, outcome, stakeholder, implementation state, and value the customer originally intended to achieve.
Cross-functional
Capture signals outside Customer SuccessSales, Product, Engineering, implementation, Renewals, and Support often hold pieces of the customer picture. The operating system should make those pieces available before a blind spot becomes a surprise.
Build pattern

Standardize the common work. Escalate the exceptions. Learn from both.

Onboarding
Milestones, owners, cadence, definition of doneReduce ambiguity early so customers and internal teams know what completion looks like.
Health
Signals tied to decisionsAdoption, support severity, engagement, commitments, stakeholder behavior, sentiment, and renewal timing only matter if they lead to action.
Renewal
Evidence before negotiationI do not lead with the upgrade. I lead with the unmet need. When evidence shows a real gap, expansion becomes a logical next step rather than a sales push.
Escalations
One customer narrative, clear internal ownershipEstablish impact, bring in the right specialists, communicate transparently, drive the decision path, and close the loop after resolution.
Post-incident
Turn the fire into a system improvementDocument what failed, where the signal first appeared, what coordination was missing, and what should change so the same issue is caught earlier next time.
Operations
Make the work inspectableUse playbooks, CRM hygiene, reporting, automation, handoff rules, and named ownership to reduce repeated coordination work while preserving judgment for edge cases.
