CUSTOMER SUCCESS SYSTEMS

Customer Success should make the next action obvious.

I build lightweight operating systems that keep customer objectives, adoption, risk, commitments, renewal timing, and ownership visible. The goal is not more process. The goal is fewer surprises.

Core motion

Six operating questions I want answered for every account.

01 · Outcome

What is the customer trying to accomplish?

Keep the definition of done visible from implementation forward.

02 · Adoption

Are they using what they bought?

Measure meaningful usage and workflow adoption, not activity for its own sake.

03 · Risk

What can derail the relationship?

Technical, commercial, stakeholder, support, and expectation risk should produce named actions.

04 · Stakeholders

Who matters to the outcome?

Map champions, decision makers, technical owners, detractors, and executive sponsors.

05 · Renewal

What evidence supports the renewal?

Renewal readiness should accumulate throughout the relationship, not begin at the deadline.

06 · Next action

Who owns what happens next?

Every meaningful risk or commitment should have an owner, date, and visible follow-up.

Build pattern

Standardize the common work. Escalate the exceptions.

Onboarding
Milestones, owners, cadence, definition of doneReduce ambiguity early so customers and internal teams know what completion looks like.
Health
Signals tied to decisionsAdoption, support severity, engagement, commitments, sentiment, and renewal timing only matter if they lead to action.
Renewal
Evidence before negotiationI do not lead with the upgrade. I lead with the unmet need. When evidence shows a real gap, expansion becomes a logical next step rather than a sales push.
Escalations
One customer narrative, clear internal ownershipEstablish impact, bring in the right specialists, communicate transparently, and close the loop after resolution.
Operations
Make the work inspectableUse playbooks, CRM hygiene, reporting, automation, and handoff rules to reduce repeated coordination work.
