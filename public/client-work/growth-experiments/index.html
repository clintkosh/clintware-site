CLIENT WORK · GROWTH EXPERIMENTS

Test channels. Measure signal. Build what works.

I use focused growth experiments to find out which audiences, messages, channels, and systems can create measurable movement for a business. I start small, instrument the test, learn from real behavior, and expand only where the signal supports it.

Start a growth experimentBack to Client Work

Start with one useful hypothesis.

A growth experiment does not need to begin with a full campaign calendar or activity on every platform. It can begin with one audience, one message, one channel, and a measurable question.

HYPOTHESIS → TEST → MEASURE → LEARN → EXPAND

TikTok may be the first channel when short-form discovery fits the business. X, LinkedIn, landing pages, referral systems, or other distribution can follow when the evidence supports the next test.

What I can test.

Content + Message
Find what earns attention.Test themes, hooks, formats, positioning, and calls to action against real audience response rather than assumptions.
Distribution
Find where the signal travels.Start with the most plausible channel, then adapt proven material into additional channels only when there is a reason to do it.
Measurement
Track more than follower count.Baseline and measure audience growth, reach, engagement, retention, profile activity, link clicks, site traffic, leads, referral activity, conversion, and attributable revenue where the systems permit it.
Attribution
Connect activity to outcomes where possible.Use campaign-specific links, UTM parameters, platform analytics, site analytics, and affiliate attribution such as AWIN when an actual eligible affiliate relationship exists.
Automation
Turn repeatable wins into a system.When a test produces useful evidence, I can help make the workflow easier to repeat through templates, lightweight tooling, automation, or a more durable operating process.

A bounded first engagement.

A typical first Growth Experiment can run as a defined pilot: capture the baseline, choose the hypothesis, publish and test, measure the response, iterate on evidence, and finish with a concise readout of what changed and what should happen next.

BASELINE → TEST → ITERATE → ATTRIBUTION → FINDINGS

The goal is not to create activity for its own sake. The goal is to produce evidence about what is worth repeating, changing, automating, or stopping.

Why this sits under Client Work.

Growth Experiments are selective service work, not a new Clintware product and not a generic marketing-agency offer. I use the same operating approach I use elsewhere in Clintware: define the problem, build the smallest useful intervention, instrument it, and make the next decision from evidence.

Patterns discovered through client work may inspire future tools, but client information, proprietary material, and engagement-specific work remain separate from Clintware-owned product development.
