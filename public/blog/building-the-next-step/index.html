Building the Next Step

How an unexpected career reset became a period of active creation, and why Clintware is where experience, practical AI, and what comes next meet.

August 4, 2026 · By Clint Kosh

In April, I was unexpectedly laid off.

At first, the experience was not only about finding another job. It was about resetting, taking stock of more than 20 years of experience, and figuring out where I fit in a professional world being reshaped by AI.

I began asking different questions.

What kind of work do I want to do next? Which parts of my experience are most valuable now? Where can I combine Customer Success, technology, implementation, operations, and AI in a way that creates something useful?

Somewhere between those questions and the search for my next opportunity, I started building.

From job search to active creation

The job market has changed quickly.

AI now influences how people research roles, prepare applications, write résumés, practice interviews, evaluate opportunities, and demonstrate their capabilities. At the same time, employers are still determining what AI adoption should look like inside their businesses.

The tools are advancing faster than the operating models around them.

Companies need more than access to AI. They need workflows, testing, governance, measurement, adoption, and people who can translate technical capability into something customers and internal teams can actually use.

That is the type of environment where I do my best work.

I have spent much of my career entering situations where the process was not fully defined, the signals were scattered, and someone needed to create structure. AI has given me a new set of tools for doing that work.

RenewNudge

One of the first projects to come from this period was RenewNudge.

RenewNudge grew from a challenge I had seen repeatedly in Customer Success: organizations often have plenty of customer information, but struggle to turn it into timely renewal action.

Usage, engagement, support activity, customer sentiment, executive alignment, contract timing, and unresolved issues may all exist somewhere. The problem is that they are often spread across systems and viewed without a consistent framework.

RenewNudge became my way of exploring how those signals could be brought together, weighted, and translated into a clearer picture of risk and opportunity.

Building it allowed me to examine practical questions:

  • Which customer signals are most meaningful?
  • How should renewal risk be scored?
  • What makes an alert useful rather than distracting?
  • How can AI support professional judgment without replacing it?
  • How can teams move from reactive renewal management to a more proactive process?

RenewNudge is still evolving, but it changed the direction of my work.

It moved me from simply studying AI to applying it to a business problem I already understood deeply.

What Clintware is

That process eventually led to Clintware.

Clintware is my personal creation brand for the tools, software, services, and experiments I am developing.

It is where I can bring together practical ideas, build working prototypes, test new workflows, document lessons, and share solutions that may be useful to other people and organizations.

Some projects may become software products. Others may become consulting services, internal tools, demonstrations, experiments, or starting points for a larger solution.

The common thread is practical problem-solving.

Clintware is not meant to suggest that every project is complete or that every idea has already become a commercial business. It is a place to show the work as it develops.

It allows me to demonstrate how I think, what I build, how I approach unfamiliar problems, and how I connect technology to real operational needs.

More than a portfolio

I think of Clintware as more than a traditional portfolio.

A portfolio often shows the final result. Clintware can also show the process behind it.

It can document why a tool was created, what problem it attempts to solve, which assumptions were tested, what failed, what improved, and what could come next.

That matters because the future of AI work will not be defined only by polished demonstrations.

It will be defined by the ability to take an unclear problem, build something useful, test it against reality, listen to feedback, and improve it.

That is the capability I want Clintware to represent.

A hybrid stage of growth

I currently find myself in a hybrid stage between experienced operator, active learner, builder, consultant, and job candidate.

I am bringing more than 20 years of experience across Customer Success, cybersecurity, SaaS, implementation, customer operations, and technology-enabled service delivery into a field that requires constant learning.

I understand adoption, customer relationships, escalation management, operational design, renewal risk, service delivery, and measurable outcomes.

At the same time, I am continuing to develop deeper experience with AI platforms, APIs, automation, prototyping, and software development.

I do not see those as separate paths.

The real value is in combining them.

AI adoption will require people who understand both the technology and the conditions in which people are expected to use it. It will require people who can connect new capabilities to existing workflows, customer needs, business priorities, and measurable results.

That is where I see my next step.

What comes next

I am still looking for the right full-time opportunity.

I am especially interested in roles where the environment is still being built, workflows need to be designed, tools need to be tested, and teams need help moving from AI experimentation to practical adoption.

At the same time, I will continue building Clintware.

Future posts will explore RenewNudge in more detail, including the original idea, the customer signals behind it, the design decisions I made, and what I learned from building it.

I will also share more about the job search, AI adoption, Customer Success operations, workflow transformation, software experiments, and the broader process of determining what success looks like in a world increasingly shaped by AI.

The past several months have not been about starting over.

They have been about combining what I already know with what I am now learning.

Clintware is where those two paths come together.
