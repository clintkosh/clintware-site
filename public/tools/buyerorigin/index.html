BuyerOrigin

Identity and promotion eligibility for commerce, starting with a narrow merchant problem: the same buyer repeatedly appearing as “new” after superficial identifiers change. The product question is whether eligibility can be recognized reliably enough to protect promotions without turning normal customers into fraud suspects.

The wedge

Merchant problem
Acquisition promotions can be repeatedly consumed by the same underlying buyer.Email addresses, account names, cookies, and other obvious identifiers are easy to change. The merchant pays an acquisition incentive without necessarily acquiring a new customer.
Product idea
Separate promotion eligibility from one superficial account identifier.BuyerOrigin explores a merchant-facing identity/eligibility layer that can reason about whether an offer is genuinely new-customer eligible while minimizing unnecessary personal-data collection.
Hard part
Reliability and privacy matter more than catching every abuse case.A system that incorrectly blocks legitimate households, shared devices, family members, or privacy-conscious users creates a worse merchant problem. The identity model has to be conservative and explainable.

What still has to be proven

Demand
Merchants need to rank this problem high enough to pay for a solution.The size of discount leakage and the operational pain need direct merchant evidence.
Signal quality
Eligibility decisions must remain useful without becoming invasive.The product needs a privacy-aware set of signals with known confidence and an appeal/override path.
Integration
The first implementation has to fit normal commerce workflows.Shopify or another narrow platform wedge may be useful, but it should be chosen because merchants can test it quickly, not because “platform integration” sounds like product progress.

BuyerOrigin remains an exploratory track. Quillgeist and LandThePlane are the current Fall 2026 YC application candidates.
