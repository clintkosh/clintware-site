STARTUP · CURRENT FOCUS

Building BuyerOrigin.

BuyerOrigin helps merchants decide whether an acquisition offer is actually available to a shopper based on the merchant's own customer-history rules. It separates identity and offer eligibility from payment authorization so repeat buyers do not automatically qualify as new customers.

Open BuyerOriginProduct details

What works now.

Audit
Local merchant-data review.The current MVP can ingest transaction/customer data and identify repeat-buyer patterns relevant to acquisition-offer eligibility.
Identity rules
Configurable matching rather than one brittle identifier.Merchants can define identity thresholds across available customer signals instead of treating payment authorization as proof of new-customer status.
Offer policy
Merchant-controlled eligibility.Lookback window, acquisition-wide or same-code scope, allowed prior uses, and merchant overrides are explicit policy inputs.
Explainability
Decisions remain inspectable.Eligibility results expose the relevant reason and can be reviewed rather than hidden behind a black-box score.
Economics
Leakage can be estimated and exported.The audit connects identity and eligibility findings to potential acquisition-offer leakage for merchant review.

What I am proving next.

01 · Real data
Run BuyerOrigin against outside merchant data.Confirm that the repeated-customer patterns exist in real acquisition programs, not only test data.
02 · Economic value
Have merchants confirm actual leakage.Measure whether BuyerOrigin finds enough preventable acquisition spend to matter operationally.
03 · Decision quality
Measure overrides and false positives.Eligibility logic has to remain useful without creating unacceptable friction for legitimate shoppers.
04 · Willingness to pay
Convert proof into a merchant commitment.Paid validation is stronger evidence than feature interest. Integration and enforcement expand only after the underlying value is proven.
