Objective

Rescue a stranded group at a procedural settlement and deliver them to a designated safe pad before a moving zombie horde reaches the pickup zone.

Why it fits

Rotorwash already models settlements, cargo/weight, fuel, weather, day/night visibility, landing zones, tactical views, and mission contracts. The new layer can reuse those systems instead of creating a separate arcade mode.

Pressure

The horde advances across terrain toward survivor noise/visibility. Landing too far away costs time; landing too close risks rotor/airframe damage or a failed pickup window. Survivors add real payload mass once aboard.

Success

All required survivors reach the safe pad. Score can combine lives saved, aircraft condition, fuel efficiency, elapsed time, and optional difficulty modifiers such as rain, night operations, or reduced assist.

Failure

The horde reaches the group, the aircraft becomes unflyable, fuel is exhausted without a survivable landing, or the required survivor threshold is not extracted.

EXPANSION PATH

Mission framework first, additional levels second.

Level 02

Night rooftop pickup

Use landing light and NVG systems to extract survivors from a constrained roof while streets below become unsafe.

Level 03

Storm evacuation

Heavy rain, wind, reduced visibility, and a longer extraction route make flight planning as important as the pickup.

Level 04

Split rescue

Two groups, limited fuel, and payload constraints force the pilot to choose sequence, refuel strategy, and risk.

IMPLEMENTATION BOUNDARY

Preserve Rotorwash's simulation identity.

The contribution should use the existing flight model, weight/fuel consequences, procedural world, visibility systems, and contract structure. Zombies create mission pressure; they should not replace the aircraft simulation with a different game. The first implementation milestone is a reusable mission-state layer: spawn objective → advance threat → open pickup window → attach survivor payload → evaluate extraction → score outcome.
