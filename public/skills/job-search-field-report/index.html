Job Search Field Report ASTRO

A recurring job-search operating report that tracks exactly what changed since the last report was sent, defines the market you are actually competing in, refreshes current labor-market evidence, and shows how your funnel is performing relative to that environment.

The rule that prevents report drift

difference_window = (latest_sent_report_at, current_at]

A draft does not advance the comparison clock. The next report begins when the previous report was actually sent, whether that interval is six hours, nine days, or three weeks.

More than a tracker

SegmentDefine the market

Role family, seniority, industry, geography, work model, and compensation are separated so the report judges the market the candidate is actually searching.

OutlookRefresh current conditions

Official labor data leads, platform and industry research adds detail, and recent Reddit/practitioner discussion captures hiring friction that statistics often miss.

Relative progressRead the funnel in context

Conversion and velocity are compared with credible evidence when possible. If no valid percentile exists, the report uses a transparent descriptive band instead of making one up.

What it tracks

  1. Cumulative application and interview funnel totals.
  2. Exact since-last-sent advances, closures, applications, interviews, referrals, and follow-ups.
  3. Market segment and current labor-market outlook.
  4. Screen, live-interview, panel, final-stage, offer, and active-signal conversion.
  5. Market-relative strengths and bottlenecks.
  6. An evidence-backed active pipeline rather than every open application.

Evidence rules

Official sources carry the most weight. LinkedIn/Indeed/industry research provides a second layer. Reddit is intentionally included for qualitative hiring-cycle and candidate-experience signals, but it remains anecdotal and never becomes a fake population statistic.

Original briefing design

The report uses the general strengths of a modern technology digest: a compact masthead, strong hierarchy, short summaries, clear section labels, restrained accent colors, and fast-scanning source notes. It deliberately does not copy another newsletter's logo, exact palette, typography, signature wording, or section structure.

Public samples remove private names, email addresses, real hiring companies, personal job-search metrics, and private project details. The skill does not imply Y Combinator or any newsletter-brand endorsement.
